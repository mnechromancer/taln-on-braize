# Unbroken: Taln on Braize
### Narrative Bible — v1.1 (Approved)

*Phase 1 deliverable. Consolidates canon, game-original fiction, and the boundary between them. Design Doc v1.0 is the parent document; where the two disagree, the Design Doc governs mechanics and this document governs fiction. v1.1 (2026-09-25): peakspren name decided (§5.2, §10 decision 3).*

---

## 0. How to Use This Document

Three tiers of authority, marked throughout:

| Tag | Meaning | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **[CANON]** | Published Sanderson canon or Word of Brandon. | Do not contradict. If the game needs to bend it, it becomes [INVENTED] and gets logged in §9. |
| **[EXTRAPOLATED]** | Not stated in canon, but consistent with it and unlikely to be contradicted. | Safe to build on. Re-check if new Stormlight material lands. |
| **[INVENTED]** | Original to this game. | Fully ours. Must not be presented to players as canon. |

Anything written for player-facing text — item flavor, room descriptions, Agony-vision fragments, death lines — must be traceable to an entry in this document. If it isn't here, it isn't approved.

## 1. Premise in One Paragraph

Taln is the Herald of War, the only one of the ten who never broke. **[CANON]** At Aharietiam the other nine, worn down by three thousand years of cyclical torture, abandoned the Oathpact and left him to hold it alone; because one is a number of power in the cosmere, one Herald was enough to keep the lock closed. **[CANON]** He has held it for four and a half thousand years, dying and waking and dying again on a lightless prison world, with no witness and no relief. **[CANON]** The game is that duration made playable. **[INVENTED]**

The fiction's core assertion: **Taln's endurance is not passivity.** Canon shows him as the object of torture. This game makes him its subject — a man who, sometime in that four and a half millennia, stopped merely surviving Braize and started fighting it, with two gifts from a defecting Unmade and nothing else. **[INVENTED]**

## 2. Taln — Character Bible

### 2.1 Who he was before

**[CANON]** Born on Roshar over seven thousand years before the Night of Sorrows — **the only Herald not from Ashyn.** His grandmother was born on Ashyn and came over in the Exodus; he blamed Honor for Ashyn's destruction.

**[CANON]** A soldier and bodyguard. Not a king, not a scholar, not a general — the only Herald who was none of those things. Nale proposed him as the tenth after Hoid refused; Nale wanted someone who understood the common man and who would argue back. Tanavast didn't like the idea.

**[CANON]** Before the Oathpact he attempted to kill Cultivation and failed; the attempt warped his soul. He received a weapon from Kalak and lost it. He was dismissed from the front lines, forbidden a weapon, and became a horse keeper.

**[CANON]** He initially refused Honor's offer, then asked the price. When he learned the suffering involved — and that someone else would be chosen if he declined — he accepted, so that no one else would have to. He swore to protect the people. **He did not swear his oath to Honor.**

> *"I will protect the people of this land, I will hold back the darkness. Not for Honor. But I'll do it."* — his oath, canon

That last clause is the character in miniature and should be the tonal spine of every line of player-facing writing in this game. He is not doing this for a god. He does not like the god. He is doing it because the alternative is that someone else does.

### 2.2 Who he was as Herald

**[CANON]** Patron of the Order of Stonewards; embodied resolve, strength, dependability. Associated with the number 9 (tanat), the Essence of Talus, and the Divine Attributes *Dependable* and *Resourceful*. Titles: Herald of War, Herald of Soldiers, Bearer of Agonies, Stonesinew, Ancient of Stones, Great Prince.

**[CANON]** The most formidable fighter among the Heralds without dispute; in his prime, likely nobody in the cosmere could beat him one-on-one. Trained humanity's soldiers between Desolations. Repeatedly chose to die achieving objectives thought impossible. Soft-spoken and caring. When men fight and die, nothing could keep him from fighting beside them.

**[CANON]** Cognitive Shadow, ageless, resurrects on death and returns to Braize. Accessed Cohesion and Tension through his Honorblade, powered directly by Honor rather than by Stormlight. Honor later granted the Heralds greater access to the Surges and to the power of Roshar itself.

### 2.3 Who he is in the game — the emotional throughline

Placement: **during the four-and-a-half-thousand-year solitude**, after the others left, before the return to Kholinar. **[INVENTED]**

Canon brackets the period at both ends: coherent, self-sacrificing man going in; broken, mantra-repeating wreck coming out. **[CANON]** The game occupies the middle and is, structurally, the story of how the second state was earned. This is the single most important framing decision in the project: **the player is not playing toward victory, they are playing toward the man from the Way of Kings epilogue.** Every meta-progression gain is bought with a piece of him.

Four movements, to be paced across meta-progression tiers rather than across a single run: **[INVENTED]**

1. **Duty.** Early cycles. He is lucid, methodical, still counting. Still assumes the others will come back. Voice: a soldier's after-action report.
2. **Realization.** He stops expecting them. Does not break; recalculates. This is where "Welcome the Agony" first becomes a thing he *chooses* rather than endures.
3. **Instrumentality.** The long middle. He has become a machine for holding. Language flattens. He starts referring to himself in the third person, or by title.
4. **Erosion.** Late tiers. Fragments of the Kholinar mantra start surfacing in Agony-visions, out of order, spoken by him to no one. The player recognizes them before he does.

**Design constraint:** the game must never frame his endurance as triumphant in the sports sense. Pillar 1 — endurance, not victory — is a writing rule as much as a systems rule. No victory fanfare. No "you win." The best possible outcome is *still holding.*

**[CANON]** Upon finally learning of the betrayal, Taln's reaction was joy — that humanity had been given four thousand years to prepare. Not rage. This is essential and easy to get wrong. Whatever bitterness the game gives him must be aimed at Honor, at Odium, at the situation — **never at the nine.** He forgives them in canon. Any line that reads as resentment toward Jezrien, Ash, or the others is off-model.

**Decided: the ending gestures at Kholinar implicitly only.** No cutscene of the return, no playable epilogue, no explicit "and then he ran to warn them." The game ends inside the cycle; Kholinar is where the player's own knowledge of canon takes over, not where the game takes them. This governs the Phase 6 ending and tutorial/onboarding work.

### 2.4 Voice guide

| Trait | Example register | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Plain, concrete, soldierly | "The ground here is wrong. It'll hold." | Ornate, poetic, self-dramatizing |
| Understated about pain | "That one was bad." | Describing his own suffering at length |
| Instructional by reflex | Explains things to nobody, as if training recruits | Lecturing the player directly |
| Dry, rarely | "Nine of them. One of me. Math favors the room." | Quippy, modern, ironic |
| Third person when far gone | "The Bearer of Agonies is still standing." | Grandiose self-reference while lucid |

He does not curse by his own name (Rosharans do: "Taln's palms," "Taln's nails" **[CANON]** — good fodder for loading-screen flavor from an in-world editorial voice, never from him).

## 3. Braize — Setting Bible

### 3.1 Canon facts, non-negotiable

**[CANON]**
- Third planet from the sun, beyond Roshar. Cold, barren, inhospitable — habitable, but barely.
- **No biological life on the surface.** Broken rocky landscape, crags and chasms.
- **No light sources. The sky is dark at all times.** Weak atmosphere. Possible strong winds and flashes that may be lightning — though that vision was Odium-mediated and may have been embellished.
- The only inhabitants are self-aware spren and the Fused. Only Odium's spren are welcome; for anything else, arriving is difficult and dangerous.
- A "storm" of some kind surrounds Braize in Shadesmar.
- **Metallic core that inherently attracts Investiture and souls** — a "well of souls." This is *why* Braize was chosen as the prison.
- Braize is built around the **number nine**, not ten, unlike everything else in the Rosharan system.
- The Heralds' souls function as the lock. While they remain, Odium's forces cannot reach Roshar. The Fused hunt and torture them to make one give up.
- Known to Vorinism as **Damnation**, destination of the gravest sinners. Referred to by its true name in older songs.

> Raboniel on Braize, paraphrased: a barren place, dark sky, endless windswept crags, broken landscape — and a great many souls, most of them not sane.

### 3.2 Correction to the Design Doc

**Decided:** Design Doc §4's "distant pinpoint sun" is struck. Canon says the sky is dark at all times and there are no light sources. Braize has no sun, full stop — light comes only from Investiture. **Every light source in the game is magic, hostile, or Taln himself:** Voidlight violet, the peakspren's molten cracks, gemheart glow, the Honorblade. Nothing ambient. Nothing warm. Art Direction doc proceeds on this basis.

### 3.3 Braize as antagonist **[INVENTED]**

The design doc's "the arena is built to torture him" needs a mechanism, and canon supplies an elegant one: **the metallic core attracts souls and Investiture.** Braize is a drain. Everything Invested slides toward it and accumulates.

From that, three invented properties, each load-bearing for a system:

1. **The Well.** Ambient Investiture is scavengeable because it *pools* here — canon-grounded justification for the in-run Investiture resource with no Stormlight and no highstorms. **[EXTRAPOLATED]**
2. **Accretion.** Four and a half thousand years of a soul-attracting core, and a great many not-particularly-sane souls **[CANON]**. Terrain on Braize is partly built *out of* what the core has pulled in. Crags that were things. The arena is haunted in a literal, Realmatic sense. **[INVENTED]**
3. **The Nine.** Braize's nine-centricity is the arena's organizing grammar. **[INVENTED]** **Decided: structural, not decorative.** Nine areas, nine breakers in the rotating cast, nine-sided geometry in architecture, a Cataclysm budget that reads in ninths — pick as many of these as the Core Systems Spec and Art Direction doc can carry, but the number nine is load-bearing wherever Braize's own design shows through. The player should eventually notice that this place counts wrong — because the number that governs it is Odium's, not Honor's.

### 3.4 Area brief — naming and tone **[INVENTED]**

Areas are not "levels." They are **stations of the cycle** — the Fused rotate him through them. Naming convention: plain descriptive Alethi-flavored compounds, not fantasy proper nouns. *The Shelf. Ninefold Descent. The Quiet Field. Where He Kept the Count.* Avoid anything that sounds like a videogame zone name.

Each area should answer one question: **what does this place take from him?** Sleep, footing, memory, silence, the ability to see his own hands. That answer drives its hazard design and its breaker.

## 4. Sja-Anat

### 4.1 Canon

**[CANON]** One of the Unmade, a Splinter of Odium; **Taker of Secrets**. Sapient, self-aware, responsible for her own actions — regarded as an individual where other Unmade are regarded as forces. The Unmade most feared by the Radiants, specifically for corrupting spren.

Her process is **Enlightening**: injecting spren with Voidlight. Enlightened spren carry Odium's taint, change in color and shape while remaining recognizable, and in rare cases gain Odium-associated abilities. She calls them her **children** and wants to find them a home she can share. She historically Enlightened lesser spren; she has since Enlightened true spren — and **does not usually Enlighten true spren without their consent.** Enlightening has dire ramifications for existing Nahel bonds, so she works with unbonded spren.

She wants out from under Odium. She deceives him successfully and repeatedly. She wants to be a god in her own right. She exists between the Physical and Cognitive Realms, sees both as shadowy, and appears in the Physical only through reflected light — mirrors, or fleeting glimpses in shadow. Her messages are spoken through an Enlightened spren, in her voice, directly into the recipient's mind.

**[CANON]** After Aharietiam she mostly *slumbered*, finding it hard to think without her bond to Odium, and was only revitalized when the Everstorm appeared in Shadesmar centuries before the True Desolation.

### 4.2 Lore tension and resolution — *read this one*

The Design Doc places Sja-Anat's visit "before Taln's return to Braize." Two problems:

- **Timing.** Taln returns to Braize at Aharietiam. For the visit to precede it, it happens *before or during* Aharietiam — the period immediately preceding her long dormancy. Workable, and arguably better: it explains why she never followed up.
- **Reach.** Braize is hostile to anything not aligned with Odium, and she is a Splinter *of* Odium — so she can plausibly reach it. But everything she does is under his nose, and Taln is the lock keeping Odium's own forces contained. Helping him is the most dangerous thing she could possibly do.

**Recommended resolution [INVENTED]:** the visit happens in the last days of Aharietiam, on Roshar, while the Oathpact is collapsing and Odium's attention is entirely on the Heralds' betrayal — the one window where an Unmade could do something unobserved. She gives him the two gifts, tells him she cannot come again, and goes dormant. **He does not hear from her for four and a half thousand years.** He does not know whether she survived. He does not know whether she meant it.

This is strictly better fiction than a benefactor who checks in. It also protects the design: no Sja-Anat deus ex machina, no vendor NPC, no guidance system. She is a memory and a debt.

**Decided: she does not make contact again anywhere in the game.** No return visit, no late-game reveal that she's been watching. The debt stays unpaid and unexplained. This is a hard ceiling on any future narrative content involving her.

### 4.3 Her motive, as the game plays it **[EXTRAPOLATED]**

Canon-consistent and already correct in the Design Doc: she is experimenting. Every successful Enlightening is practice for the abilities she will need to avoid being enslaved again — by Odium or anything else. Taln is an ideal subject: maximally Invested, maximally isolated, and located where nobody is looking for good news.

But there is a second motive the game should carry, because it is more interesting and equally consistent: **she is testing whether Enlightenment can be given to a willing subject and leave him himself.** Consent is canonically her line. Taln — a man who agreed to four thousand years of torture with full knowledge of the price — is the most informed consent available anywhere in the cosmere. **[INVENTED]**

### 4.4 Rules for writing her

- Voice: formal, slightly archaic, declarative, unsettlingly sincere. Short sentences. She states things. She does not persuade.
- **She is never on-screen in the present.** Only in the opening, and in Agony-visions and fragments. If she appears, it is in reflection — a gem's facet, a wet stone, the flat of a blade.
- She is not a villain and not an ally. She is a third party with her own war.
- Never make her warm. Her care for her children is real and reads as ownership.

## 5. The Peakspren in the Gem

### 5.1 Canon on peakspren

**[CANON]** True spren. Bond humans into **Stonewards**, granting Cohesion and Tension — exactly Taln's Surges, which is why this is the right choice and not a convenience. In the Physical Realm they appear made of stone and can hide inside real rock, emerging as if breaking out of it. In the Cognitive Realm they inhabit humanoid stone bodies of every variety of Rosharan stone; skin etched with cracks that emanate molten light, sometimes forming shapes like glyphs — though only warm to the touch. Bald, heads more cracked than bodies. Voices like grinding rock; they move heavily.

**[CANON]** Culturally they resemble the Unkalaki — speech patterns, mannerisms, naming conventions. They use gendered pronouns for emphasis. They have tribes and rivalries. They are opinionated and non-homogeneous: some argue for joining the war against Odium, others for sitting it out and staying useful.

**[CANON — and a gift]** *The arrival of a peakspren has been known to give Taln a few lucid moments.* This is canon. Use it. **In this game, the spren in the gem is the thing that keeps pulling him back to himself.** The mechanical "unpredictable Voidlight" tool and the narrative "only voice that reaches him" are the same object, and that tension is the whole character.

### 5.2 The character **[INVENTED]**

**Decided (Phase 2, 2026-09-25): Toa'uma.** Full poem-name *Toa'uma'haliki'ka'amu'a*; pronounced toh-ah-OO-mah. Built on Unkalaki conventions **[CANON]**: an agglutinated poem-name cut to a short form from its head, apostrophes as glottal stops, and two attested roots, *toa* (Physical) and *liki* (Cognitive). Gloss **[INVENTED]**: "stone-body kept only in thought; heavy where there is no ground" — named for a body he does not have. In late tiers (Instrumentality, Erosion), Taln shortens it to "Uma." The gloss is never stated in player-facing text; the name carries it for players who know the roots.

Design:

- **Enlightened, and consented to it.** Per canon, Sja-Anat does not usually Enlighten true spren without consent. He agreed. He does not entirely remember agreeing to *this much*. **[INVENTED]**
- **Gem-bound, not manifest.** He has no stone body here — Braize will not give him one. The molten cracks that should run through a peakspren's skin instead run through the gem's facets. He is a mountain with nowhere to stand.
- **Voice:** grinding, slow, blunt, clipped. Drops articles. Declarative. Unkalaki-flavored. *"Ground is soft. You will fall. I said."*
- **Personality:** stubborn, proud, bored, and increasingly not-right. Peakspren argue **[CANON]**; this one argues with Taln constantly and is usually correct.
- **The eldritch edge:** the Voidlight in him has been working on him for four thousand years with no Sja-Anat to check it. He drifts. Mid-to-late meta-progression, his lines should start acquiring a second register underneath the first — the same sentence meaning two things, one of them Odium's. He never becomes an enemy. He becomes *unreliable about who is speaking.* **[INVENTED]**
- **The relationship:** this is the only relationship in the game. It is two prisoners who have been in the same cell for four and a half thousand years. They are not friends. They cannot be separated. Write it like that.

### 5.3 Why the Honorblade is the safer option, in fiction

Sja-Anat's advice in the Design Doc — prefer the Blade, the spren is unpredictable — is well-founded and should be voiced in-world: **the Blade is Honor's, and Honor is dead but his Investiture is inert and obedient. The spren is Odium's now, partly, and Odium is very much awake.** Drawing Voidlight on Braize is drawing on the local god's own power inside the local god's own prison. Of course something notices. **[EXTRAPOLATED]**

## 6. The Ashynite Vessel

### 6.1 Canon on Ashyn

**[CANON]** First planet from Roshar's sun; formerly *Alaswha*. Odium arrived and granted its people power over Surges — far more powerful than what Roshar's singers had. Roughly nine hundred years of escalating conflict ended in a cataclysm that burned most of the planet; the name changed to Ashyn in reference to the burning. Survivors fled to Roshar as refugees roughly seven thousand one hundred years before the True Desolation, using Elsecalling amplified by Ishar's Bondsmith powers. Odium came with them.

**[CANON]** **After the Scouring, Ashyn's magic changed to become illness-based.** A symbiotic relationship between Investiture and bacteria/viruses: the microbes keep their hosts alive and grant abilities in exchange, and Ashynites deliberately lower their immune systems and infect themselves. The ability lasts as long as the infection does and ends when the immune system wins. Known effects: one disease causes hearing loss while granting gravity manipulation — this is what keeps the floating cities aloft; others grant flight or passing through walls.

**[CANON, unpublished — *The Silence Divine*, treat as soft]** Abilities come with side effects: physical weakness, hallucinations, deadly fevers. Symptoms that persist after the disease resolves are called **Echoes**. Carriers are called **Incubators**.

### 6.2 Lore tension and resolution — *read this one too*

**The Design Doc's premise has a chronology problem.** Canon strongly implies the shift to disease-based magic happened *after* the Scouring and largely after the evacuation — the Eila Stele describes the refugees as using spren and Surges, not diseases. So "a disease-culture Taln carried over from the Exodus" is not straightforwardly supported.

Three resolutions, in order of preference:

1. **Recommended — the grandmother line. [INVENTED, canon-anchored]** Canon gives Taln an Ashyn-born grandmother and gives him alone among the Heralds a Rosharan birth. Make the vessel *hers*: brought over in the Exodus, or sent after, or recovered later by a family that never stopped being Ashynite. This grounds the vessel in the single most personal canon detail Taln has, converts a lore problem into characterization, and explains why he kept it for millennia when it did nothing useful. **It is the only thing he owns that is his rather than the Oathpact's.**
2. **Late arrival.** The culture came to Roshar after the main Exodus, during the period when Ashyn's magic was already shifting — smuggled, traded, or carried by a later refugee. Cleaner chronologically, weaker emotionally.
3. **Proto-culture.** What he carries predates the shift and is an ancestor of Ashyn's current magic — Sja-Anat's Enlightening is what pushed it over into a working system. Makes her experiment more consequential; requires the most invention.

**Decided: Option 1, the grandmother line.** The rest of this document assumes it, and the Ashynite tree and vessel art in later phases build on it as settled fact, not a placeholder.

### 6.3 The vessel **[INVENTED]**

- A small sealed container — ceramic or glass, Ashynite-made, unmistakably *not* Rosharan in silhouette. Art Direction should treat it as the only object in the game that looks like it came from a living civilization.
- Contents: a single culture, kept alive for thousands of years by a man who does not sleep and cannot die permanently. **Feeding it is one of the ways he has spent the time.** This is characterization, not flavor text: for millennia the only living thing on Braize besides him was a disease, and he kept it alive.
- Sja-Anat Enlightened what was already there rather than introducing something foreign **[per Design Doc]** — consistent with her method and with her consent rule, since the culture was his.

### 6.4 Framing for the Ashynite tree

Mechanics are backlogged per Design Doc §9, but the **fiction** needs guardrails now so the eventual system doesn't have to be retrofitted:

- **Infection is a bargain, not a curse.** Canon: the microbes keep the host alive because they want to live. Every Ashynite ability should read as a negotiation — the boon is what it gives you, the bane is what it needs from you.
- **Duration is the natural resource cost.** Canon: the ability lasts until the immune system clears it. Taln's body is a Cognitive Shadow's and does not behave normally, which is precisely why he can carry things that would kill a person — and why the clearing never quite finishes. **[EXTRAPOLATED]**
- **Echoes are the meta-progression hook.** Symptoms that persist after the disease resolves **[canon, soft]** map cleanly onto permanent between-run effects. Recommend Echoes become the Ashynite tree's signature mechanic rather than inventing something new.
- **Never make it body horror for its own sake.** The tone is medical, patient, and slightly tender. He is a curator.

## 7. The Other Nine, the Oathpact, and Roshar

### 7.1 Presence rules

The other nine are **narrative-only** and **never appear** — no cameos, no boss forms, no voice lines. **[Design Doc constraint]** They exist as:

- **Memory fragments** in Agony-visions: training a recruit with Jezrien, arguing with Nale, being given a weapon by Kalak and losing it **[CANON]**.
- **Absence made physical.** Their abandoned Honorblades **[CANON]** are on Roshar, not here. Nine empty positions on a line he is holding alone.
- **The count.** He knows when one of them dies and returns to Braize, and knows they are not staying. **[EXTRAPOLATED from canon mechanics]** Recommend this become a periodic, non-interactive event: something arrives, and then it is gone, and he keeps going. Never explained by a tutorial.

### 7.2 What he knows about Roshar

**Almost nothing, and that is the point.** He does not know four thousand years are passing. He does not know the Knights Radiant will fall on the Day of Recreance. He does not know he was left deliberately until he is told, in canon, much later.

**Design implication:** lore unlocks about Roshar are for the *player*, not the character. Frame them as Agony-visions — things that reach him distorted and which he does not reliably retain. The player accumulates a picture of a world that he never gets. **[INVENTED]**

### 7.3 The Oathpact, plainly

**[CANON]** Honor bound Odium to the Rosharan system. The Oathpact binds the Heralds and the Fused to Braize. While the Heralds remain, Odium's forces cannot reach Roshar; when one breaks and leaves, the Fused follow and a Desolation begins. Departure is difficult and scales with how Invested the prisoner is: minor voidspren can slip out even mid-torture; Fused and thunderclasts only once the Heralds leave; Odium's most powerful servants take a long time even after a Desolation is underway.

**[EXTRAPOLATED, important for encounter design]** This gives the roster a free internal logic. The Fused on Braize are not an invading army — **they are fellow inmates whose release depends on breaking him.** The enemies want the same thing the player's failure state would deliver. Every miniboss is a negotiator with fists.

### 7.4 Enemy roster principles **[per Design Doc + INVENTED]**

- **No reskinned canon Fused.** Original invented variants only. **[Design Doc constraint]**
- Nine brands is the right instinct for Braize's nine-centricity **[CANON adjacency]** — recommend the invented Fused roster be organized in nine, not ten.
- **Breakers** are the moment another Herald would have broken. Name each after the pressure it applies, not after a creature: *the Patient One, the Accountant, the One That Waits Out the Storm.* **[INVENTED]**
- Voidspren on Braize are the only spren welcome here **[CANON]** — they should read as staff, not wildlife. Bureaucrats of the prison.

## 8. Themes and Writing Rules

**Primary theme:** duty without audience. Every theatrical virtue — glory, witness, legacy — is unavailable to him. What remains is the thing itself.

**Secondary theme:** the cost of being load-bearing. He is a lock. Locks do not get to rest, and locks are not asked.

**Tertiary theme:** the two gifts. Both are compromised, both came from an enemy's servant, both are the only kindness he has received in four thousand years. Gratitude and contamination are the same feeling here.

**Hard rules:**
1. No fourth-wall breaks. No modern idiom. No irony.
2. The player is never addressed by the fiction. UI text is a separate, neutral register.
3. Nothing in-world ever confirms that anyone will come. Hope is the player's to supply, or not.
4. No romance, no companion-bonding arc. The peakspren is not a buddy.
5. Suffering is stated, never dwelt on. One clause, then move.
6. He never breaks. Not in a cutscene, not in a bad ending, not in a joke. **This is the premise; there is no version of the game where it is subverted.**
